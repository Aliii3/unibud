# Unibud — Privacy Policy

**Last updated: 12 October 2026**

## The short version

Unibud does not collect anything. There is no account, no server, and no
analytics. Everything you put into the app stays in a database on your
device, and the app makes no network requests at all.

The one exception is something you have to ask for: tapping "Add to
calendar" or "Add to Reminders" on a deadline hands a copy of that one
entry to Apple's Calendar or Reminders app. What happens to it then is up
to how your calendar is set up — see Permissions below.

## What we collect

Nothing. Unibud has no sign-up, no login and no user profile. We do not
collect, transmit, sell or share personal data, usage data, device
identifiers, location, contacts or advertising identifiers.

## What the app stores, and where

Everything you enter — subjects, class times, deadlines, tasks, documents
you attach and the chapter names you give them — is written to a SQLite
database in Unibud's private storage on your device.

Documents you attach are **copied** into that private storage so the app can
still open them if the original moves. Those copies are also only on your
device.

None of it is uploaded, backed up to us, or readable by us. We have no
servers, so there is nowhere for it to go.

If your device backs itself up (iCloud Backup or Android's backup service),
app data may be included in that backup. That is handled by Apple or Google
under their own privacy policies, not by us.

## Permissions

Unibud asks for three things, and only when you use the feature that needs
it.

**Notifications** — so it can send the daily check-in and remind you before
a deadline. These are *local* notifications, scheduled by the app on your
device. There is no push server and nothing is sent anywhere. Declining
simply means no reminders; the rest of the app works normally.

**Files** — when you attach a document, the system file picker opens.
Unibud only receives the specific file you choose. It cannot browse your
storage.

**Calendar and Reminders** — only if you tap "Add to calendar" or "Add to
Reminders" on a deadline. Nothing is exported automatically and nothing is
kept in step afterwards: a copy is handed over once, and the two stop being
related.

Be aware of what that means. Unibud keeps your coursework on your device,
but a calendar usually does not stay there — if yours syncs to iCloud, a
Google account or anything else, the entry you added will travel with it,
under that provider's privacy policy rather than this one. That is the
system calendar working as intended, and it is outside what Unibud can see
or control. If you would rather nothing left the device at all, do not use
these two buttons; everything else in the app works without them.

iOS grants calendar access as a single permission. Unibud uses it to read
the list of your calendars, so it knows where to put the entry, and to add
the entry you asked for. It does not read your existing events, and it never
changes or deletes anything already in your calendar.

## Third parties

There are none. Unibud contains no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK, no
crash reporting service and no social login. No third party receives data
from this app because the app does not send data.

Apple and Google collect their own information about app downloads and, if
you have opted into sharing them, crash reports. That happens at the store
and operating-system level and is governed by their policies.

## Children

Unibud is rated for all ages and collects no data from anyone, including
children under 13. Because nothing is collected, there is nothing to obtain
parental consent for.

## Keeping or deleting your data

Your data lives on your device, so you control it. Deleting a subject
removes its documents, chapters, tasks and deadlines with it. Deleting the
app removes the database and every attached document copy permanently.

We cannot delete your data for you, because we never have it. For the same
reason we cannot recover it if you delete the app, and there is no export or
restore.

## Changes

If the app ever starts handling data differently — for example by adding
optional sync — this policy will be updated before that version ships, and
the date at the top will change.
